Product & SaaS Blueprint · v1.0

Voya

A booking and fleet platform built for how West Africa actually moves — intercity buses, shared taxis, and okada, on networks that drop and phones that run low on storage.

Passenger app Operator SaaS USSD + agent network Mobile money native

Why this, why now

01

Formal online booking exists for airlines and a handful of premium intercity lines. Everything below that — union buses, shared taxis (sept-place, tro-tro, gbaka), and moto-taxis — still runs on paper manifests, shouted destinations at the gare, and cash held by a tout. Riders over-pay, operators under-report, and nobody has a seat guarantee.

Voya doesn't try to replace the gare. It gives the gare a ticket counter in every rider's pocket, and a dashboard for the operator who's been running the route on memory.
Market reality

Informal ≠ unplannable

Routes, departure windows, and fares are well known locally — they're just not written down anywhere a booking engine can read.

Market reality

Phones, not desktops

Android devices under 2GB RAM, intermittent 3G, and prepaid data bundles are the default environment, not the edge case.

Market reality

Cash is trusted, apps aren't yet

Mobile money (MTN MoMo, Orange Money, Wave) has crossed the trust line that card payments never did. Cash-on-boarding still has to work.

Market reality

Many languages, one receipt

French, English, and Portuguese across borders; Wolof, Twi, Yoruba, Mooré, Bambara in speech. The ticket itself has to read clearly to everyone.

What a booking actually does

04

The full path from search to boarding, across the three surfaces.

Search route & date→ Hold seat (90s lock)→ Pay — MoMo / Wave / cash-to-agent→ Offline-readable e-ticket (QR + 6-digit code)→ Driver scans or keys code at boarding→ Manifest & payout update live
Feature

Seat holds, not just bookings

A 90-second soft lock on a seat while payment completes prevents double-selling on routes where three people are booking the same last seat at once.

Feature

Tickets that work with no signal

The boarding pass is a signed QR plus a 6-digit fallback code, cached on-device, verifiable by the driver's phone without a network call.

Feature

Hybrid cash flows

A seat can be reserved online and paid in cash to the agent at boarding — reservation and settlement are decoupled, which is how most of the market already thinks about paying.

Feature

Route-level trust score

On-time departure rate and rider ratings per route (not just per operator) surface the lines that actually leave when they say they will.

Feature

Diaspora booking

A relative abroad can book and pay for a parent's seat at home; the ticket ties to the traveler's phone number, not the payer's.

Feature

Cargo & parcel slots

Many coaches already carry parcels between cities informally — a parcel booking reuses the same seat-manifest logic as a line item, with a separate pickup code.

Design principles

05

Soft UI here means calm, legible, and forgiving of bad connectivity and bright sunlight — not a visual trend.

Principle

One primary action per screen

Every screen has exactly one obvious next step, sized for a thumb, legible at arm's length in direct sun.

Principle

State survives the network

A search, a held seat, a half-filled form — all persist locally, so a dropped connection costs time, never progress.

Principle

Numbers before prose

Price, departure time, and seat count render before any decorative content loads — the decision-critical data never waits behind an image.

Principle

Language is a setting, not a market split

French, English, Portuguese, and the four most-requested local languages share one interface — switching never changes what a feature does.

Principle

Receipts people can act on

Every confirmation (SMS, in-app, printed) names the plate number, the departure point, and a human contact — never just a transaction ID.

Principle

Errors name the fix

"Your seat hold expired — search again" instead of a code. Every failure state is written by someone who has missed a bus.

Security & trust

06

Money, identity, and a seat on a moving vehicle are all on the line — security here is a product feature riders can feel, not just a compliance checkbox.

PCI

No card or wallet data touches Voya's servers

Payments route through licensed mobile-money and card PSPs (Flutterwave, Paystack, direct MoMo APIs); Voya stores only a payment reference and status.

AES

Tickets are signed, not just generated

Each QR/code is a signed token (operator ID, seat, trip, expiry) verified offline with a rotating public key cached on the driver's device — a photo of a ticket can't be reused on another date.

KYC

Operators are verified before they can list a route

Business registration, vehicle roadworthiness document, and a bank or mobile-money settlement account are checked before any route goes live — riders book from a vetted list, not an open marketplace.

2FA

Fleet dashboard access is scoped and logged

Dispatchers, drivers, and finance roles get least-privilege access; every manifest edit, refund, and payout is attributed and auditable.

GDPR/DPA

Data residency respects local law, not just EU habit

Rider PII is stored in-region (aligned with Nigeria's NDPA, Ghana's Data Protection Act, and ECOWAS data frameworks as each market goes live) with a minimal retention window after a trip completes.

ANTI

Fraud signals are route-aware

Velocity checks on holds, device-fingerprint limits on free promo credit, and anomaly flags on an agent's cash-ticket ratio catch the specific abuse patterns an informal-transit marketplace actually sees.

Architecture at a glance

07

Built to degrade gracefully — a core booking flow that still completes when a secondary service is down.

Rider app / USSD gateway / Fleet dashboard→ API gateway (rate-limited, signed requests)→ Booking service→ Ledger & payout service
Payment service→ MoMo / Wave / Card PSPs
Notification service→ SMS / WhatsApp / push, region-local gateways
Data

Postgres, region-sharded

One primary region per country cluster; booking and ledger data never crosses borders at rest.

Resilience

Async settlement

A payment confirmation can lag the PSP by seconds without blocking the rider's ticket — the ticket issues on a provisional hold, confirmed server-side moments later.

Edge

USSD as a first-class client

The USSD gateway talks to the same booking API as the app — it is not a separate, lagging product.

Offline

Local-first ticket cache

Issued tickets, held seats, and the operator's public verification key sync to the device and work with zero connectivity at the boarding gate.

How we'll know it's working

10
Rider

Repeat booking rate

Share of riders who book a second trip within 60 days — the real test of trust, above download counts.

Operator

Manifest accuracy

Gap between seats sold in Voya and seats actually filled at departure — the number that proves touts aren't still skimming.

Agent

Cash-ticket settlement time

Hours between a cash sale and its reconciliation in the ledger — speed here is what keeps agents honest and paid on time.

Platform

On-time departure rate

The single stat that decides whether Voya becomes the default way to check a route, not just book one.
